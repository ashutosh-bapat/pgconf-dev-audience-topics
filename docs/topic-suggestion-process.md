# Community topic request process

This repository is an experiment in community-driven topic discovery for pgconf.dev.

## Goal

The goal is to help organizers understand what potential attendees most want to learn, discuss, and see represented in the conference program.

## How topic requests work

1. Community members create discussions in the **Topic Requests** category.
2. Others can comment, refine the idea, and upvote topics they support.
3. Organizers review the discussions to identify themes, demand, and opportunities.
4. For selected topics, organizers may reach out to possible speakers and encourage formal submissions through the normal pgconf.dev process.

## What organizers look for

Organizers may consider:
- number of upvotes and overall engagement
- clarity and usefulness of the proposed topic
- fit for pgconf.dev attendees
- balance across the overall program
- whether the idea is timely or underrepresented
- whether a suitable speaker is available

## Important limitations

Topic support in GitHub Discussions is a signal of interest, not a guarantee that a talk will appear in the final schedule.

Some highly requested topics may not make the program because of:
- limited schedule space
- overlap with other accepted talks
- lack of an available speaker
- mismatch with the event format
- program balance considerations

## Formal submissions

Formal speaker proposals and final session selection continue to happen through the usual pgconf.dev process.

This repository does not replace the normal CFP or selection workflow. It helps surface demand and identify good candidates for outreach.

## Suggested speakers

Community members may suggest speakers in discussion threads. Suggested speakers should be treated as ideas, not commitments.

Organizers should confirm interest and availability directly with any suggested speaker before assuming participation.

## Community expectations

Please keep discussions constructive, specific, and respectful.

When possible:
- search for existing topics before posting
- add support to existing discussions instead of creating duplicates
- use comments to improve topic framing and scope
